How to Create an ATS-Friendly Resume

Learn how to organise your resume, choose relevant keywords, and keep the layout simple, so applicant tracking systems can easily scan your information.

Updated August 2026 · 8 min read

In this guide

Many companies use applicant tracking systems, or ATS software, to sort, organize, and review job applications more efficiently.

An ATS helps employers handle large numbers of applications and quickly find resumes that include relevant skills and information for a specific job.

Making your resume ATS-friendly goes beyond adding keywords. Focus on a clean layout, intuitive structure, and relevant language so both software and recruiters can easily read it.

What Is an ATS?

Employers use applicant tracking systems to streamline how they collect, organize, and manage job applications.

Depending on the ATS and the employer, your resume details may be sorted into different fields, making it easier for recruiters to search and review your information.

Important: There’s no single resume format that guarantees it will work perfectly with every ATS. Different systems and employers may read and process resumes in different ways.

Why ATS-Friendly Formatting Matters

A resume can look great but still be hard for ATS software to read if the layout is too complicated.

Keeping your resume design straightforward ensures that critical details stand out to software and recruiters alike.

The goal should therefore be to create a resume that is:

  • Easy to read
  • Clearly structured
  • Relevant to the job
  • Consistent in formatting
  • Easy to scan

Choose a Simple Resume Format

Start with a clean layout instead of a complicated design.

A straightforward resume can include:

  1. Contact information
  2. Professional summary
  3. Work experience
  4. Education
  5. Skills
  6. Additional relevant sections

The exact order can vary depending on your background and the type of position you are applying for.

Use Standard Section Headings

Clear and familiar section headings make your resume easier to understand.

Examples include:

  • Professional Summary
  • Work Experience
  • Education
  • Skills
  • Projects
  • Certifications

Avoid replacing standard headings with unnecessarily creative names.

Example

Instead of using a heading such as "My Professional Journey" , use:

Work Experience

Use Relevant Job Keywords

Tailoring a resume effectively comes down to using precise terminology that aligns with the role's key requirements.

Read the job description carefully and pick out the key skills, technologies, qualifications, and responsibilities that genuinely match your experience.

Example

Suppose a job description requests:

  • SQL
  • ETL
  • Data analysis
  • Tableau

If you have those skills, make sure they’re clearly mentioned in the relevant sections of your resume.

Remember: Never add skills, technologies, or qualifications you don’t actually have. Tailoring your resume means highlighting your real experience, not simply copying keywords from the job description.

Write ATS-Friendly Experience Sections

Your experience section should clearly show where you worked, your role and what you accomplished.

A useful structure is:

Job Title
Company Name · Location
Month Year – Month Year

Use short, clear bullet points to highlight your key responsibilities and achievements.

Example

Data Analyst
ABC Technologies · Kolkata
January 2024 – June 2026

  • Created SQL queries to analyze business data and prepare useful reports.
  • Created Tableau dashboards to monitor key performance indicators.
  • Automated repetitive data tasks to make recurring reporting workflows faster and more efficient.

Whenever possible, highlight the results you achieved instead of simply listing your day-to-day responsibilities.

Format Your Skills Clearly

Keep your skills section simple and easy to scan.

Example

Technical Skills: SQL, Python, Tableau, Excel, Git

Data: Data Analysis, ETL, Reporting, Data Visualization

Use terms and keywords that accurately reflect your actual skills and experience.

Avoid using visual skill bars like these.

SQL ★★★★★

Python ★★★★☆

A simple text-based skills list is easier to read and gives recruiters more useful information.

Keep Your Resume Design Simple

An ATS-friendly resume can still look professional without being plain or boring.

You can use clean typography, balanced spacing, and a simple visual hierarchy while keeping the overall structure easy to follow.

Be careful when using:

  • Complex tables
  • Large decorative graphics
  • Text embedded inside images
  • Excessive columns
  • Decorative symbols replacing text
  • Unusual section layouts

ATS software can work differently from one system to another, so a simple and straightforward layout is usually a safer choice when ATS compatibility matters.

Choose the Right File Type

Always follow the employer's instructions when choosing the file format for your application.

If the job posting asks for a PDF, submit a PDF. If it asks for another format, use the format they specify.

Before submitting your resume, open the final file and make sure the text, spacing, and page layout look correct.

Common ATS Resume Mistakes

1. Using a Complicated Layout

Multiple columns, decorative elements, and unusual layouts can make your resume harder for ATS software to process and recruiters to review.

2. Keyword Stuffing

Using the same keywords too often will not make your resume stronger. Instead, use relevant terms naturally when describing your actual skills and experience.

3. Using Images for Important Information

Keep important information as regular text instead of placing it inside an image or graphic.

4. Using Unclear Section Names

Use familiar headings such as Experience, Education, and Skills to make your resume easier to understand.

5. Sending the Same Resume Everywhere

A generic resume may not clearly show why you are a good fit for a specific position.

6. Adding Skills You Do Not Have

Only include qualifications and technologies that genuinely match your skills, knowledge, or experience.

Final ATS-Friendly Resume Checklist

Before submitting your resume, make sure you have checked the following:

  • The resume has a clean and easy-to-read layout.
  • Standard section headings are used.
  • Your contact information is easy to find.
  • Relevant keywords from the job description are used naturally.
  • Your experience is described using short, clear bullet points.
  • Your skills are listed as text.
  • Important information is not placed inside images.
  • Spelling and grammar have been checked.
  • The resume is tailored to the specific position.
  • The final file follows the employer's submission instructions.

Final Thoughts

An ATS-friendly resume should focus on clear, relevant, and accurate information.

Use a simple structure, familiar headings, relevant keywords, and concise descriptions of your experience.

Most importantly, tailor your resume to the specific job you are applying for instead of trying to optimize it for a universal ATS score.

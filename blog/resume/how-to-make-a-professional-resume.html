How to Make a Professional Resume in 2026

Learn how to create a clear, professional and job-ready resume with the right structure, content and formatting.

A resume is one of the most important documents in a job application. It gives employers a quick overview of your background, education, skills, experience and professional strengths.

A good resume does not need to be complicated. The most important thing is to present relevant information in a clear and organized way so that employers can quickly understand your qualifications.

Whether you are a fresher, an experienced professional or someone changing careers, the structure of your resume should match your background and the type of position you are applying for.

Quick tip: Before writing your resume, read the job description carefully and identify the qualifications, skills and experience that are relevant to the position.

What Is a Professional Resume?

A professional resume is a concise document that presents your qualifications and relevant experience for a specific job opportunity.

A well-organized resume normally includes sections such as contact information, a professional summary or objective, work experience, education and skills.

Additional sections such as projects, certifications, achievements or languages can be included when they provide useful information for the position.

Essential Sections of a Professional Resume

A resume should contain the information that helps an employer understand your suitability for the position. The exact sections can vary depending on your experience.

1. Name and Contact Information

Start your resume with your name and relevant contact information.

  • Full name
  • Professional email address
  • Phone number
  • City and country or location
  • LinkedIn profile, when relevant
  • Professional portfolio or website, when relevant

Avoid adding unnecessary personal information that is not relevant to the job application.

2. Professional Summary

A professional summary provides a short overview of your background, strengths and career focus.

Keep it concise and tailor it to the position you are applying for. Focus on relevant skills, experience and the value you can bring to the role.

3. Work Experience

If you have professional experience, describe your previous roles in reverse chronological order, starting with your most recent position.

Include your job title, company name, location when useful, employment dates and a few relevant accomplishments or responsibilities.

Whenever possible, describe results rather than simply listing routine duties.

4. Education

Include your relevant educational qualifications, normally starting with the most recent degree or qualification.

  • Degree or qualification
  • Institution name
  • Graduation year or expected graduation year
  • Relevant specialization, when useful

5. Skills

Add skills that are relevant to the position. Avoid creating a very long list of unrelated abilities.

For technical positions, you can organize skills into categories such as programming languages, databases, software, tools and technologies.

6. Projects

Projects can be particularly useful for students, recent graduates and candidates applying for technical or creative roles.

Briefly explain what you built or worked on, the tools or technologies used and the result or purpose of the project.

7. Certifications

Include certifications that are relevant to the job. Mention the certification name, issuing organization and completion year when appropriate.

How to Make a Resume for Freshers

Freshers may not have extensive professional experience, but they can still create a strong resume by focusing on education, skills, projects, internships, certifications and relevant achievements.

A fresher resume can include:

  • Career objective or professional summary
  • Education
  • Technical or professional skills
  • Academic or personal projects
  • Internships
  • Certifications
  • Relevant achievements

For freshers: Do not add fake work experience simply to make your resume look longer. Use genuine projects, internships, coursework and skills instead.

What Is an ATS-Friendly Resume?

Many organizations use Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) to help manage job applications. An ATS may process resume content before a recruiter reviews the application.

There is no single format that guarantees that a resume will pass every ATS. However, a simple and clearly structured resume can make the information easier for both software and people to interpret.

Tips for an ATS-Friendly Resume

  • Use clear and standard section headings.
  • Keep important information in normal text rather than placing it inside images.
  • Use consistent formatting throughout the document.
  • Include relevant terminology from the job description when it accurately describes your experience.
  • Avoid unnecessary decorative elements.
  • Check the final document before submitting it.

Choose the Right Resume Format

Different resume formats work better for different career situations.

Chronological Resume

A chronological format emphasizes work experience and normally lists positions from most recent to oldest. It can be useful for candidates with a consistent career history.

Functional Resume

A functional format focuses more heavily on skills than employment history. It may be useful in certain career situations, although recruiters often prefer clear employment information.

Combination Resume

A combination format brings together relevant skills and work experience. It can be useful for candidates who want to highlight both technical abilities and professional experience.

How Long Should a Resume Be?

The appropriate resume length depends on your experience, career stage and the requirements of the position.

For many early-career candidates, a concise one-page resume may be sufficient. Candidates with substantial relevant experience may need additional space.

The goal should be to include relevant information without adding unnecessary content simply to increase the number of pages.

Resume Design and Formatting Tips

A professional resume should be easy to read. Good formatting helps recruiters find important information quickly.

  • Use a readable professional font.
  • Maintain consistent heading styles.
  • Use sufficient white space.
  • Keep margins consistent.
  • Use bullet points for easy scanning.
  • Avoid excessive colors or decorative graphics.
  • Keep dates and formatting consistent.

Common Resume Mistakes to Avoid

Even a well-qualified candidate can weaken a resume through avoidable mistakes.

  • Spelling and grammatical errors
  • Using an unprofessional email address
  • Adding irrelevant information
  • Creating extremely long paragraphs
  • Using inconsistent formatting
  • Listing skills without evidence or context
  • Including false or exaggerated information
  • Using the same resume for every job without considering the position

How to Tailor Your Resume for a Job

A general resume can be a useful starting point, but tailoring it for a specific position can make the relevant information easier to identify.

Start by reviewing the job description. Identify the required skills, responsibilities and qualifications. Then make sure your resume clearly presents the experience and skills you genuinely have that relate to those requirements.

Do not add keywords or skills that you do not actually possess. Your resume should accurately represent your background.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should a professional resume include?

A professional resume commonly includes contact information, a summary, work experience, education and relevant skills. Projects, certifications and other sections can be added when they are useful for the position.

How do I make a resume with no work experience?

Freshers can focus on education, skills, projects, internships, certifications, coursework and relevant achievements instead of adding unrelated or false work experience.

Should a resume be one page?

A one-page resume can work well for many students and early-career candidates. More experienced professionals may need additional space to present relevant accomplishments.

What is an ATS-friendly resume?

An ATS-friendly resume uses clear structure, readable formatting and relevant text that can be processed easily by applicant tracking systems and understood by recruiters.

Should I customize my resume for every job?

It is useful to review your resume for each position and emphasize the qualifications and experience that are genuinely relevant to that particular job.

Can freshers use a resume builder?

Yes. A resume builder can help organize sections, maintain consistent formatting and create a structured resume.

Conclusion

Creating a professional resume is mainly about presenting relevant information clearly and accurately. A strong resume should make it easy for an employer to understand your qualifications, experience and skills.

Choose a suitable format, keep the content relevant, check your writing carefully and tailor the resume to the position whenever appropriate.

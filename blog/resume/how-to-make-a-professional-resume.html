How to Make a Professional Resume

Learn how to create a professional resume with a clear structure, relevant information and simple formatting that makes your qualifications easy to understand.

Updated August 2026 · 8 min read

A professional resume is more than a list of jobs and qualifications. It is a structured document that presents your experience, education, skills and achievements in a way that is easy for recruiters to scan and understand.

A well-organized resume can help you present your strongest qualifications clearly while keeping the document concise and relevant to the position you are applying for.

What Is a Professional Resume?

A resume is a short professional document that summarizes your background, including your work experience, education, skills and other relevant qualifications.

A professional resume should make it easy for someone to quickly understand who you are, what you can do and why your background may be relevant to a particular role.

Important: A good resume does not need to contain every detail about your career. Focus on information that is relevant, useful and easy to verify.

Choose the Right Resume Structure

Before writing your resume, decide how you want to organize your information. The most common structure for many job seekers is the reverse-chronological format.

In this format, your most recent experience is presented first, followed by older positions. This allows recruiters to quickly see your latest responsibilities and achievements.

A simple professional resume can usually include the following sections:

  • Contact information
  • Professional summary or objective
  • Work experience
  • Education
  • Skills
  • Relevant certifications or additional sections when appropriate

Add Your Contact Information

Your contact information should be easy to find at the top of your resume.

Depending on your situation, this section can include your full name, phone number, professional email address, location and relevant professional links.

Keep your email professional

Use an email address that looks professional and is easy to read. Avoid using usernames that may appear informal or unrelated to your professional identity.

Add relevant professional links

If you have a professional website, portfolio or LinkedIn profile that supports your application, you can include it in the contact section.

Write a Strong Professional Summary

A professional summary is a short introduction that explains your background, key strengths and career focus.

Instead of writing a generic statement, focus on your experience, technical or professional strengths and the type of work you can contribute to.

Keep it concise

A summary of a few focused sentences is usually easier to read than a long paragraph. Remove information that does not help explain your professional value.

Tip: Write the summary after completing the rest of your resume. This makes it easier to identify your strongest qualifications.

Describe Your Work Experience

Work experience is one of the most important sections of a resume. List your relevant positions in reverse-chronological order, starting with the most recent role.

For each position, include the job title, company name, location when relevant and employment dates.

Focus on achievements and responsibilities

Instead of listing only routine duties, explain what you worked on and what you accomplished whenever possible.

  • Describe important projects you worked on.
  • Mention measurable results when reliable numbers are available.
  • Highlight tools, technologies or processes relevant to the target role.
  • Keep older or less relevant experience concise.

Use clear bullet points

Short bullet points are generally easier to scan than large paragraphs. Begin each point with a clear action or responsibility and explain the result when appropriate.

Add Your Education

Include your relevant educational qualifications in a separate section.

You can include the degree or qualification, institution name, location and completion year when appropriate.

Students and recent graduates may provide additional academic details, such as relevant coursework, projects or academic achievements, when those details strengthen the application.

Highlight Relevant Skills

The skills section should focus on abilities that are relevant to the position you are targeting.

Avoid filling the section with unrelated skills simply to make the resume appear longer.

Technical skills

Depending on your field, technical skills may include programming languages, software, databases, design tools, analytics platforms or other job-related technologies.

Professional skills

Communication, organization, problem-solving and teamwork can be relevant, but they are more useful when supported by examples from your experience.

Use Professional Formatting

Good formatting makes your resume easier to scan. You do not need complicated graphics or excessive design elements to create a professional document.

  • Use clear section headings.
  • Keep spacing consistent.
  • Use a readable professional font.
  • Keep font sizes consistent throughout the document.
  • Use bullet points for important details.
  • Avoid excessive colors and decorative elements.
  • Keep alignment consistent.

A clean layout can make the content easier to understand and can also help automated systems process the information correctly.

Common Resume Mistakes

Using the same resume for every job

A generic resume may not highlight the qualifications most relevant to a particular position. Review the job requirements and emphasize experience and skills that genuinely match the role.

Adding too much information

More information does not automatically make a resume stronger. Remove outdated, repetitive or irrelevant details.

Using inconsistent formatting

Different fonts, inconsistent spacing and irregular headings can make a resume look unfinished.

Including unsupported claims

Avoid adding achievements, qualifications or skills that you cannot accurately explain or support during the hiring process.

Forgetting to proofread

Spelling mistakes, incorrect dates and inconsistent information can reduce the professional quality of a resume. Review the final document carefully before sending it.

Final Resume Checklist

Before submitting your resume, check each important part of the document.

  • Your name and contact information are correct.
  • Your email address looks professional.
  • Your most relevant experience is easy to find.
  • Your education information is accurate.
  • Your skills are relevant to the target role.
  • Dates and job titles are consistent.
  • Formatting is consistent throughout the document.
  • The resume has been checked for spelling and grammar errors.
  • The final file opens correctly before sending or uploading it.

How to Make a Professional Resume

Learn how to create a professional resume with a clear structure, relevant details, and simple formatting that helps employers quickly understand your skills and experience.

Updated August 2026 · 8 min read

A professional resume is more than just a list of jobs and qualifications. It is a well-structured document that highlights your experience, education, skills, and achievements in a way that is easy for recruiters to read and understand.

A well-organized resume helps you highlight your strongest qualifications while keeping the content clear, concise, and relevant to the position you are applying for.

What Is a Professional Resume?

A resume is a concise professional document that gives an overview of your background, including your work experience, education, skills, and other relevant qualifications.

A professional resume should make it easy for recruiters to quickly see who you are, what you can do, and how your background relates to the role you are applying for.

Important: A good resume does not have to include every detail of your career. Focus on information that is relevant, useful, and easy to verify.

Choose the Right Resume Structure

Before you start writing your resume, decide how you want to organize your information. For many job seekers, the reverse-chronological format is a simple and effective choice.

With this format, your most recent experience appears first, followed by your earlier positions. This helps recruiters quickly see your latest responsibilities and achievements.

A simple professional resume will usually include these sections:

  • Contact information
  • Professional summary or objective
  • Work experience
  • Education
  • Skills
  • Relevant certifications or other sections when appropriate

Add Your Contact Information

Keep your contact information easy to find at the top of your resume.

Depending on your situation, you can include your full name, phone number, professional email address, location, and any relevant professional links.

Keep your email professional

Choose an email address that looks professional and is easy to read. Avoid usernames that may seem too casual or unrelated to your professional identity.

Add relevant professional links

If you have a professional website, portfolio, or LinkedIn profile that supports your application, you can add it to the contact section.

Write a Strong Professional Summary

A professional summary is a brief introduction that highlights your background, key strengths, and career focus.

Rather than using a generic statement, highlight your experience, professional or technical strengths, and the type of work you can bring value to.

Keep it concise

A few focused sentences are usually easier to read than a long paragraph. Leave out details that do not help show your professional value.

Tip: Write your summary after finishing the rest of your resume. This makes it easier to identify and highlight your strongest qualifications.

Describe Your Work Experience

Work experience is one of the most important parts of your resume. List your relevant positions in reverse-chronological order, starting with your most recent role.

For each position, include your job title, company name, location when relevant, and employment dates.

Focus on achievements and responsibilities

Instead of simply listing your daily duties, highlight what you worked on and the results or achievements you delivered whenever possible.

  • Describe the important projects you have worked on.
  • Include measurable results when you have reliable numbers to share.
  • Highlight tools, technologies, or processes that are relevant to the role you are applying for.
  • Keep older or less relevant experience brief.

Use clear bullet points

Short bullet points are easier to scan than long paragraphs. Start each point with a clear action or responsibility, and mention the result when it adds useful context.

Add Your Education

Include your relevant educational qualifications in a separate section of your resume.

You can list your degree or qualification, institution name, location, and completion year when relevant.

Students and recent graduates can also include relevant coursework, projects, or academic achievements when these details help strengthen their application.

Highlight Relevant Skills

Focus your skills section on abilities that are relevant to the position you are applying for.

Avoid adding unrelated skills just to make your resume look longer or more detailed.

Technical skills

Depending on your field, technical skills may include programming languages, software, databases, design tools, analytics platforms, or other technologies related to the job.

Professional skills

Skills such as communication, organization, problem-solving, and teamwork can be valuable, especially when you can support them with examples from your work experience.

Use Professional Formatting

Good formatting makes your resume easier to scan and understand. You do not need complicated graphics or too many design elements to create a professional-looking document.

  • Use clear section headings.
  • Keep the spacing consistent.
  • Choose a professional font that is easy to read.
  • Keep font sizes consistent throughout the resume.
  • Use bullet points to highlight important information.
  • Avoid using too many colors or decorative elements.
  • Keep the alignment consistent.

A clean layout makes your content easier to read and can also help automated systems process your resume more effectively.

Common Resume Mistakes

Using the same resume for every job

A generic resume may not highlight the qualifications that matter most for a specific position. Review the job requirements and highlight the skills and experience that genuinely match the role.

Adding too much information

Adding more information does not necessarily make your resume stronger. Remove outdated, repetitive, or irrelevant details that do not add value.

Using inconsistent formatting

Different fonts, uneven spacing, and inconsistent headings can make your resume look unfinished and harder to read.

Including unsupported claims

Avoid adding achievements, qualifications, or skills that you cannot accurately explain or support during the hiring process.

Forgetting to Proofread

Spelling mistakes, incorrect dates, and inconsistent information can make your resume look less professional. Review the final document carefully before sending it.

Final Resume Checklist

Before submitting your resume, take a moment to review each important part of the document.

  • Your name and contact information are correct.
  • Your email address looks professional.
  • Your most relevant experience is easy to find.
  • Your education details are accurate.
  • Your skills are relevant to the role you are applying for.
  • Your dates and job titles are consistent.
  • The formatting is consistent throughout the document.
  • The resume has been checked for spelling and grammar errors.
  • The final file opens correctly before you send or upload it.
